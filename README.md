> *"Physics teaches you how systems actually behave — FPGA design teaches you how to make them behave on purpose."*

I'm an **FPGA RTL Design Engineer** building high-speed digital hardware at the register-transfer level, with a foundation in **semiconductor physics** that most engineers coming from a pure CS/EE-coursework background simply don't have.

My M.Sc. in Physics — with specialization in **Electronics** — wasn't a detour into hardware; it was the on-ramp. Studying **semiconductor physics, solid-state physics, electronic devices, and instrumentation** means I don't just know that a setup/hold violation exists — I understand the underlying carrier dynamics, propagation behavior, and signal-integrity physics that create it. That translates directly into stronger analytical thinking for **timing closure, noise margins, clock domain crossing, and FPGA architecture decisions** than a purely textbook-Verilog approach can offer.

Day to day, I work across the full RTL lifecycle: writing synthesizable **Verilog/SystemVerilog**, building **self-checking testbenches**, closing timing with **SDC/XDC constraints**, and debugging real silicon behavior with **SignalTap and ILA**. My core specialization is **USB 2.0/3.0 IP core development** — designing and porting hub and device controllers across **Intel/Altera, Xilinx, and Microchip** platforms, working directly with **ULPI PHY** interfaces at the signal level.

I approach every RTL bug the way a physicist approaches an anomalous measurement: question the model first, not the tool.

## `05` &nbsp;Featured Projects

<details open>
<summary><b>🔌 USB 2.0 Hub Controller</b></summary>
<br/>

FPGA-based USB 2.0 Hub Controller supporting High-Speed (480 Mbps) and Full-Speed (12 Mbps) communication, built around a ULPI PHY interface with full downstream port management.

| Category | Details |
|---|---|
| **Stack** | Verilog / SystemVerilog, ULPI PHY (USB3320), Vivado, Quartus, QuestaSim |
| **Architecture** | Multi-port hub FSM with per-port line-state detection, resume/remote-wake-up handling, and a 3-flop synchronization pipeline for cross-clock signal stability |
| **Challenges** | Resolved multi-domain 60 MHz ULPI clock timing constraints, DRC multi-driver conflicts in encrypted IP netlists, and signal integrity issues across FPGA I/O delays |
| **Performance** | Achieved stable timing closure across High-Speed and Full-Speed modes with clean STA reports across multiple vendor toolchains |
| **Engineering Impact** | Enabled cross-platform portability of a single RTL codebase across Intel, Xilinx, and Microchip FPGA families |

</details>
